# P.A.I.N. / Evidence, representation, and interpretation

Artist-team reference | Advanced / professor level | English
Last updated: 2026-09-09. Version: 1.0.

This reference develops the project's ideas for readers across art, medicine, environmental
science, social science, and machine learning. Specialist terms are defined because expertise in
one field does not imply familiarity with the others. The central question is what each visible
element permits us to understand, and what additional evidence a stronger claim would require.

The material supports independent explanation in the artist's own words. Country observations,
contextual evidence, and possible interpretations are distinguished. Optional formulations are
examples of emphasis, without a required order or wording.

## The project in one view

| Element | Meaning |
| P.A.I.N. | Personal And Interconnected with Nature. |
| PPP Map | Personal-Planetary-Pain Map, bringing human and environmental concerns into a shared artwork. |
| Four layers | Physical health burden; distress in language; warming and emissions; economic circumstances. |
| Current emotional display | Earlier analysis of 249,438 texts, with one prominent category per observed country. |
| Latest completed research | 26,176,468 texts across 192 observed countries, including 25,932,030 news articles. |
| Main CCNews resource | 393,745,301 country-linked records, of which 388,842,327 have recorded years 2015-2026. |

The artwork creates relations between heterogeneous indicators. It does not establish a common
unit in which physical disability, textual distress, atmospheric change, and economic output
can be added into total suffering. That distinction is compatible with the work's artistic
ambition: phenomena can matter together without having equivalent measurements.

# Project vision and the kinds of knowledge it joins

P.A.I.N. places personal experience beside the planetary conditions on which lives depend. The
project, led by Mary Maggic with collaborators in art, design, sound, data research, technology,
and the Ludwig Boltzmann Institute for Network Medicine, uses a globe as a shared space of
attention. Surface, atmosphere, words, colour, and connection make different forms of pressure
available to encounter.

## Construct, indicator, and representation

A **construct** is the wider concept of interest, such as hardship or distress. An **indicator**
is the particular observation used to approach it, such as GDP per person or a text score.
A **representation** is the form through which that observation becomes visible, such as yellow
shading or a word. These three levels answer different questions.

For example, socioeconomic hardship is broader than economic production. GDP per person is an
indicator with a precise accounting meaning. Stronger yellow is the artwork's translation of
lower values of that indicator. Neither the numerical indicator nor its colour captures every
aspect of hardship. The same distinction applies to the bodily metaphor of scars and the
atmospheric metaphor of environmental pressure.

**Construct validity** asks how adequately an indicator captures its intended concept.
**External validity** asks how far an observed relationship generalizes beyond the studied
material. A clear visual encoding can coexist with open questions at either level. Legibility
is therefore a property of the artwork, while validity depends on the evidence behind a claim.

## Units of observation and units of concern

| Layer | What supplies an observation | Wider concern |
| Physical | A geographically placed health-burden signal. | Pain, activity, participation, and care. |
| Emotional | A selected text interpreted by a classifier. | Distress, expression, and recognition. |
| Environmental | A temperature-change or emissions-related observation. | Changing environments and shared pressure. |
| Socioeconomic | Economic output divided by population. | Material circumstances and available choices. |

The person is ethically central to the artwork but is usually not the statistical unit being
counted. Map points are not patients; texts are not unique authors; a country average is not a
typical resident. Keeping these distinctions visible allows the artwork to invite empathy
without claiming access to lives that its data did not directly observe.

An optional formulation is: "The map places different kinds of evidence in relation, while
leaving the lives behind them larger than their indicators."

## Geography is part of the interpretation

A country outline makes a place recognizable, but different layers use different geographic
supports. **Spatial support** means the area or location to which an observation refers.
Population-based placement, a temperature observation, a publisher's organizational country,
and national economic output do not share the same support merely because they appear on one
globe. A compact country symbol also cannot preserve every internal spatial difference.

Small territories and islands may have little or no representation in a sampled spatial layer.
Country rosters differ between sources and can include territories or economies in different
ways. A count of economic entries therefore cannot be substituted for the number of emotional
country observations. An absent mark can arise from source coverage, geographic assignment,
or representation at the selected scale.

## Interaction changes attention and sometimes the comparison

Rotation and zoom change the viewer's position. Selecting a country or category brings a part
of the work into focus. Restricting emotional categories changes the set from which the visible
winner is chosen: a different word can appear without any underlying article changing.

Animated connections organize attention from a selected starting place. They do not provide
temporal evidence of distress spreading. The soundtrack is composed; interaction tones are
artistic responses to selected signals. Neither is a recording of a population's suffering.

The related web experience can allow optional personal contributions. This is separate from
the country datasets and the large PCAI analysis. Projection mode emphasizes the shared map
and hides the survey action. Personal disclosure is not necessary to understand the work.

## Why incomplete source details remain visible

The guide can explain what a layer represents and compare its selected relative signals.
It cannot reconstruct an original clinical rate, temperature anomaly in degrees, or emissions
total in tonnes from appearance alone. Such conversions need the specific source measure,
reference period, geographic support, and transformation. These unresolved items are stated
at the relevant layer, without inventing a measurement history from the source institution's
general documentation.

Text-source counts and their original scope receive fuller treatment below because PCAI's
preparation and interpretation are central research components of the project.

# Physical pain: burden, concentration, and aggregation

The physical layer makes the consequences of painful conditions visible through a marked
surface. It connects country-based health information with population information from WorldPop.
Population concentration is consequently relevant to strong local signals, including India's.

## Six related but distinct conditions

| Category | Conceptual distinction |
| Headache disorders | A broad group of recurring headache conditions; a source of disability in daily activity. |
| Migraine | A neurological condition within the broader headache domain, often with symptoms beyond headache. |
| Low back pain | Pain in the lower back, with potential restrictions on mobility and participation. |
| Neck pain | Pain affecting the neck and activities that depend on comfortable movement or posture. |
| Osteoarthritis | Joint disease associated with structural changes, pain, stiffness, age, and other risk factors. |
| Rheumatoid arthritis | Immune-related inflammatory disease, distinct from osteoarthritis. |

The categories overlap conceptually and can coexist within a person. Adding migraine to the
broader headache category would not yield a defensible count of different patients. General
clinical descriptions clarify the topic, while the exhibition's original health measure remains
unconfirmed. It should not be silently identified as prevalence, incidence, or disability-adjusted
life years. [C3, C5-C7]

## Counts, rates, and concentration

A **count** concerns how many events or people are represented; a **rate** relates events to
a population and usually a period. A **burden measure** may instead weight consequences, such
as activity limitation. These quantities can order countries differently. A populous location
can carry a large aggregate burden even when its residents do not have the highest individual
risk. The population-based spatial placement makes that distinction especially relevant here.

The compact country summary emphasizes a maximum local signal. It answers a question about the
strongest represented location, rather than the average condition throughout a country.
If the spatial signals are v1 through vn, this summary is conceptually max(v1, ..., vn).
It is not their arithmetic mean. A country with more observed locations also has more
opportunities to contain an extreme value.

This is an explanatory property of the representation, not evidence that dense populations
cause a painful condition. Age structure, health risks, health care, reporting, and source
modelling might contribute to real-world patterns. Their separate contributions to this
exhibition signal have not been estimated.

The artistic question remains substantive: what becomes perceptible when limits on movement,
sleep, concentration, and participation are given an external form? One possible framing is:
"The surface brings a burden into view that often remains invisible in everyday encounters."

# Environmental pain: two quantities in one atmosphere

The environmental layer combines temperature change and emissions-related pressure in a shared
atmospheric form. Their relationship is scientifically meaningful, but their measurement units
and interpretation remain different.

## Temperature change and absolute heat

A temperature anomaly expresses a difference from a reference climate. A warming comparison
therefore needs both the period of interest and its reference period. A location may remain
cold while warming substantially; another may be hot while showing a smaller change relative
to its own reference. Russia and Canada make this distinction accessible in the exhibition.

Arctic amplification provides established regional context: changes in snow and ice reflectivity
interact with atmospheric and oceanic processes to produce strong high-latitude warming.
It does not supply a complete explanation of each displayed location. The selected Berkeley
Earth comparison period and baseline are still unresolved, so the image should not be
translated into a specific temperature increase. [S9, C1]

## Emissions, concentrations, exposure, and vulnerability

**Emissions** describe releases associated with activities. **Concentration** concerns the amount
of a substance present in an environment. **Exposure** concerns contact with a person or system.
**Vulnerability** concerns susceptibility to harm. A high value in one concept does not supply
the missing measurements in the others.

Climate TRACE provides estimated emissions across activities and gases. Carbon dioxide and
carbon-dioxide-equivalent totals also differ: the latter combine gases using a specified climate
equivalence framework. The exact gas measure and period selected for the exhibition remain
unconfirmed. No visual conversion into tonnes, measured smoke, or inhaled pollution is justified.
[S10]

## Local extremes and shared processes

The country summary emphasizes a strong local signal. Russia's or Iran's strong emissions
mark therefore does not establish a ranking of national emission totals or emissions per person.
Uruguay's observed zero in the temperature-change view is a fact about this selected transformed
signal, not evidence of no climate change or environmental vulnerability.

Artistically, an atmosphere that extends beyond borders places local activities within shared
conditions. Scientifically, the image invites questions about transport, accumulation, scale,
and responsibility without having measured those relationships as network edges. Warming and
emissions can be considered together while retaining the different questions their indicators
answer.

# Socioeconomic pain: an economic proxy and its denominator

The yellow layer uses World Bank GDP per capita for 2024, measured in current US dollars.
GDP is production within an economy; dividing by population provides an output-per-person
indicator. Lower values receive stronger yellow. This introduces material circumstances into
the artwork while leaving distribution and individual experience unresolved. [S11]

## Why the logarithmic display matters

Economic output spans a large range across the selected economies. A logarithmic display
compresses that range so values at its lower end remain visually distinguishable. Equal
distances on a logarithmic axis correspond to equal ratios, rather than equal dollar differences.
The display reverses direction so lower output per person produces a stronger hardship proxy.

[[DIAGRAM:scale]]

This transformation does not convert the measure into purchasing-power parity, correct for
inequality, or estimate household poverty. Current US dollars also retain currency-conversion
effects. A familiar numerical unit can make a comparison look more comprehensive than it is:
GDP per person remains different from disposable income, median income, wealth, and access to
public services.

## Economic structure changes the meaning of an average

Luxembourg illustrates a denominator issue. Cross-border workers contribute to domestic
production while residing outside the population denominator. Its high GDP per person cannot
be interpreted as the typical salary of a resident. Burundi's low value invites attention to
economic constraints, but the map alone cannot identify their causal shares or describe how
every household lives. Monaco's weak yellow signal does not make emotional or bodily pain
logically inconsistent. [C2, C4]

There are 190 available observations and 16 missing entries in the exhibition's economic roster.
Missingness, including Yemen's selected 2024 value, is a property of this observation set.
Replacing a missing value with zero would create an artificial economic extreme. Conversely,
treating an absent yellow mark as prosperity would reverse the evidential meaning of the blank.

# Emotional categories, language, and the observer

The emotional layer presents distress categories inferred from texts. Some passages express
an author's experience; others report, quote, dramatize, or discuss somebody else's experience.
Recognizing distress in language does not identify whose emotion it is, how enduring it is,
or how common it is among residents of the associated country.

## Fourteen broad openings into the text

| Display category | Scope of meaning |
| Body pain | Language expressing bodily hurt. |
| Hurt | Emotional suffering without a more specific assigned form. |
| Eco-anxiety | Fear or anxiety connected with environmental change. |
| Uncertainty | Distress associated with instability and an unclear future. |
| Grief | Loss and mourning, including distress over unwanted change to a home environment. |
| Anger | Anger, outrage, and moral injury associated with perceived wrongs. |
| Hardship | Loss, insecurity, and difficult material circumstances. |
| Displacement | Distress associated with exile, migration, or being uprooted. |
| Trauma | Language of trauma and overwhelming experience. |
| Loneliness | Social and relationship pain. |
| Depression | Depressive distress; the research taxonomy includes more severe forms. |
| Fear | General fear, anxiety, and panic. |
| Helplessness | Perceived inability to influence circumstances. |
| Shame | Shame, guilt, and self-blame. |

These categories are an interpretive taxonomy, meaning an organized set of distinctions.
They are neither an exhaustive theory of emotion nor diagnostic assessments of populations.
Several categories may apply simultaneously. The display's single prominent word is a
reduction of that multidimensional representation.

## The 47 finer distinctions

The taxonomy refines the broad groups as follows. These labels describe what the classifier
is trained to recognize in language, not a set of independently diagnosed conditions.

| Broad group | Fine categories |
| Body pain | Acute physical pain; chronic physical pain; somatic pain distress; physical exhaustion. |
| Hurt | Sadness; general emotional distress. |
| Eco-anxiety | Climate fear; climate anxiety; climate panic. |
| Uncertainty | Future uncertainty; situational instability; loss of control. |
| Grief | Bereavement grief; grief over loss other than death; solastalgia; ecological or climate grief. |
| Anger | Anger; injustice anger; betrayal distress; moral injury. |
| Hardship | Material loss; financial or livelihood precarity; food insecurity; housing insecurity. |
| Displacement | Forced displacement; exile; loss of home or rootlessness; climate or ecological displacement. |
| Trauma | Traumatic stress; hypervigilance; acute overwhelm; shutdown or dissociation. |
| Loneliness | Loneliness; social isolation; abandonment; rejection or relationship loss. |
| Depression | Depression; hopelessness; anhedonia or emotional numbness. |
| Fear | Fear; anxiety; panic. |
| Helplessness | Helplessness; powerlessness. |
| Shame | Shame; guilt; self-blame. |

In this taxonomy, **moral injury** is distress associated with perpetrating, witnessing, or
being betrayed by moral violations. It is more specific than ordinary anger at unfairness.
**Solastalgia** concerns a changing home environment while a person remains there, distinct
from displacement. **Anhedonia** means loss of interest or pleasure; **hypervigilance** means
heightened monitoring for threat. These concepts help readers distinguish neighbouring labels.

Separate safety outputs concern suicidal-ideation language and self-harm language. They do
not assess intent, planning, imminence, or clinical risk. Four out-of-scope outputs distinguish
unsupported content types, unclassifiable noise, insufficient semantic content, and content
outside the distress domain. A derived neutral/no-pain control is not a 48th learned distress
category. These distinctions explain why 70 model outputs do not mean 70 emotional themes.

## Three language questions

The language of an article, a language hint supplied with a record, and the language chosen
for a displayed word are different things. A display translation creates access and resonance;
it does not establish that the underlying country collection was written in that language.
The 192 displayed country labels use 101 language codes and 20 scripts from a curated
lexicon. This is separate from the 100-language training roster. One selected display
language cannot represent every linguistic community within a country.

Translation between brief category names is not guaranteed to preserve every cultural or
emotional association. A shared category is useful for comparison, while **measurement
invariance**, comparable meaning and performance across groups, would require separate
evidence. Neither multilingual training coverage nor a multilingual globe demonstrates it.

An optional formulation is: "A shared word creates a point of recognition across difference;
it does not erase the differences in whose language and experience reached the map."

# PCAI: the adopted classifier and what its scores mean

PCAI prepares country-associated texts; the classifier interprets them through a taxonomy of
physical, emotional, and social distress. The adopted version 9 was finalized on 4 September
2026. It combines a frozen pretrained BGE-M3 multilingual encoder with a project-trained neural
classifier. The encoder is not trained from scratch on the PCAI examples. [S12]

The classifier is designed to classify texts in 100 languages, and its synthetic teaching
collection is multilingual across those languages. That coverage is separate from demonstrated
accuracy: the research does not establish equally reliable performance in all 100 languages.

## Representation learning and the project-specific classifier

An **embedding** is a numerical representation of a text. BGE-M3 produces a 1,024-dimensional
representation; the classifier learns associations between these representations and the
project's labels. The encoder remains fixed while the project-specific network learns.
The adopted network has a shared hidden layer of 1,024 units with a smooth nonlinear activation,
followed by label-specific outputs. A shared layer allows related outputs to depend on a
common transformation of the text representation.

There are 70 outputs: three scope decisions, 14 broad groups, 47 fine categories, two
safety-related signals, and four reasons for being outside the task. These are not 70 emotions.
The safety outputs concern language signals, not clinical assessment. Fine categories refine
broader ones, allowing a passage to express more than one specific form of distress.

The classifier sees at most 512 tokens per record. Tokens are text fragments rather than a
fixed number of words. BGE-M3 has a broader underlying capacity, but the adopted PCAI analysis
uses this shorter view. Long articles can therefore lose later qualifications, quotations,
or shifts in perspective. The share affected depends on genre and language. In the earlier
5,000-news diagnostic selection, 2,479 records exceeded this limit; that diagnostic proportion
should not be assigned to the entire main corpus.

## Scope and hierarchical composition

The model first provides scores for distress being present, no relevant pain, and content
outside the task. The three scope scores are normalized to sum to one. Broad category
scores are conditional signals multiplied by the normalized distress score. Fine-category
scores are then multiplied by the score of their parent broad category.

For text x and category k, the conceptual relationship is:

**Broad score k = normalized distress score × conditional category score k.
Fine score = parent broad score × conditional fine-category score.**

This hierarchy prevents a fine category from exceeding its parent in the returned score
structure. It also means a scope error can affect every descendant score. A model that
overassigns distress relevance can raise many downstream scores even when the finer distinctions
are otherwise useful. The synthetic training material is distress-heavy, making transfer to
general news a substantive concern.

The outputs are probability-like scores. Hierarchical multiplication is a modelling decision,
not proof of probabilistic calibration. Calibration would mean that, among comparable texts
given a particular probability, the corresponding human-labelled frequency agrees with it.
That property must be checked in the target domain.

## Scores, decisions, and visible winners

A threshold turns a continuous score into a positive decision. Scope decisions additionally
use a required separation between the leading alternatives; an uncertain result is possible.
The project's diagnostic news decoding uses these threshold and margin rules, rather than
treating the largest scope score as automatically sufficient.

Three summaries must therefore be distinguished:

- A **mean score** averages the continuous model outputs across texts.
- A **positive fraction** counts texts crossing a specified decision threshold.
- A **displayed winner** is the largest permitted country-level category score.

The display excludes no-pain and out-of-scope controls when choosing a word. At least one
control exceeds the winning pain score in 121 of the 192 current country results. Display
prominence therefore does not certify that distress dominates the texts.

The emotional country values used here are mean scores. A value of 0.25 is not a finding that
25 percent of residents are distressed, nor necessarily that 25 percent of articles crossed
a positive threshold. A single winning word can conceal several other substantial scores.
Conversely, even a low set of scores has a largest member.
The 14 broad scores are not normalized to sum to one, because several categories can apply.

varied languages and forms. Synthetic examples permit deliberate coverage of labels and
combinations, but their known intended labels do not make them equivalent to naturally occurring

## What the evaluation numbers establish

| Evaluation | Broad-category macro-F1 | Fine-category macro-F1 |
| Clean synthetic reporting test | 0.9028 | 0.7772 |
| Withheld synthetic stress half | 0.6658 | 0.6552 |

**Precision** is the fraction of predicted positives that are labelled positive; **recall**
is the fraction of labelled positives that the model retrieves. F1 is their harmonic mean:
**F1 = 2 × precision × recall / (precision + recall).** Macro-F1 gives each evaluated category
equal weight. It therefore answers a different question from a record-weighted accuracy score
or a measure dominated by common labels.

The lower stress results expose difficulty under more challenging combinations and prevalence
patterns. The stress set was deliberately constructed, so it is not a representative estimate
of the error rate in world news. Nor does a broad score near 0.90 establish that the classifier
is 90 percent correct about residents' feelings.

The training work also examines the Brier score, the mean squared difference between a predicted
probability and a binary reference label. It evaluates probability predictions, whereas F1
depends on a decision threshold. Brier scores are affected by label prevalence; a stress set
with a different base rate needs appropriate comparison. Discrimination, calibration, and
thresholded performance can improve or deteriorate separately.

## Independence has more than one meaning

The clean split groups related source identities to reduce leakage of closely related material
between fitting and evaluation. That protects against a specific duplicate-related failure.
It does not make all examples semantically independent or eliminate shared generator style.

The clean reporting test was consulted repeatedly during development. It was withheld from
parameter fitting, but it was not an untouched final assessment of the entire model-selection
process. A separate stress half was reserved as a veto after candidate selection. The broader
stress resource had itself been consulted earlier in development; splitting it later does not
erase that history. The half gives additional evidence, rather than a pristine external test.
Its split also lacks the source-group protection available for the clean data, so related
examples can cross the two stress halves.

## Transfer to real-world language

**Domain shift** means that deployment material differs from the training and evaluation
material. News reporting differs from constructed first-person distress through quotation,
negation, third-person narration, mixed topics, and uncertain speaker identity. Language
coverage may differ too. Aggregate country comparisons can inherit these changes.

The adopted classifier is used for project research; independent real-world validation remains
unestablished. The completed large-scale run demonstrates that it produced results for the
selected texts. It does not establish real-world semantic accuracy, clinical validity, or
national emotional prevalence. Stronger claims would require independently labelled real
material across sources, languages, periods, and relevant forms, with disagreement and
uncertainty reported. Synthetic success and large inference volume answer useful but different
questions.

# CCNews: from approximately 600 million to the main corpus

CCNews supplies general international news gathered through Common Crawl and prepared by
Stanford OVAL. The upstream description gives approximately 600 million articles in more than
100 languages. It already includes cleaning, deduplication, and language identification.
Its crawl-period description runs from 2016 through June 2024 and distinguishes crawl dates
from article publication dates. [S1]

PCAI's locally acquired snapshot contains 474,985,436 records. The approximate upstream figure
and exact local figure do not establish a precise count removed by the project: they describe
different scopes. Further local preparation and country selection produce the main
393,745,301-record corpus across 186 publisher countries.

## The counted stages

| Locally acquired snapshot | 474,985,436 | Actual local acquisition count. |

The main corpus also contains 4,902,974 records with recorded years 2010-2014.
**388,842,327 + 4,902,974 = 393,745,301.** These are date partitions of one collection,
not separate datasets to be combined again. Both the main corpus and its later-year subset
are reasonably described as roughly 400 million when the precise scope is also stated.

## Filtering and the arithmetic of exclusion

Initial normalization standardizes characters, whitespace, and escaped symbols so equivalent
text can be compared. Initial checks remove 906,602 duplicates and 1,640,769 records failing
supported date rules, leaving 472,438,065. Later privacy cleaning reveals seven additional
duplicate collisions, giving 472,438,058 prepared records.

Country assignment excludes 78,207,037 records: 78,030,618 unresolved assignments and 176,419
conflicts. This retains 394,231,021. Text, privacy, and record eligibility checks remove
485,075 more, followed by 645 final duplicate or conflict exclusions. The result is
393,745,301. These stages explain the exact local reduction without treating every removed
record as a duplicate or a privacy problem.

Privacy preparation masks detected email addresses and telephone numbers; later checks also
mask links and user handles. Masking usually changes a text rather than discarding it. It
reduces recoverable identifiers but does not prove complete anonymization. Eligibility also
requires adequate readable prose and consistent record information. The combined eligibility
count cannot be interpreted as a count of harmful articles.

## Selection creates a particular view of the world

The collection is general news. It does not require a pain keyword for entry; the classifier
assesses distress relevance afterward. The main corpus has no per-country cap. Its distribution
reflects publisher output, web-crawler reach, language availability, and successful country
resolution. Removing unresolved publishers can alter representation even when the assignment
rule is applied consistently.

The acquired and prepared collections contain 116 recorded language codes. That figure differs
from the classifier programme's 100 training languages and the completed news selection's
104 language or variety hints. These describe different inventories and cannot be exchanged.

Recorded dates can derive from article metadata, crawl information, or an acquisition-time
substitution when another usable date is absent. A recorded 2026 year does not prove that an
article reports a 2026 event. The main dated collection contains no 2025 entries. Initial
date eligibility was not simply a 2015-2026 filter, as the retained earlier records show.

## Prepared does not mean completely classified

The main corpus supports the broader ongoing classification programme. As of the project
records checked on 9 September 2026, a full 393.7-million-record production classification
run is not established as started. The verified completed news analysis covers 25,932,030
selected records within that corpus. No full-corpus completion percentage is claimed here.

# Aggregation: which question does a country score answer?

Aggregation defines the population of records being summarized and their influence.
Changing the weighting changes the question, even when the classifier remains the same.

## The current exhibition and the latest research differ

The current exhibition uses 249,438 texts: 5,000 news articles plus 244,438 expression records.
Its news has 59 language hints; 93.84 percent has recorded years 2015-2016. The combined
exhibition texts have 84 hints and discontinuous recorded years 2010-2025. Its difference
from newer research therefore includes source dates as well as size and weighting.
Where both source families are available, it gives the news-family average 75 percent of the
weight and the expression-family average 25 percent. Where only one family is available,
the available family supplies the result.

**Exhibition score = 0.75 × news mean + 0.25 × expression mean, where both exist.**

The latest completed analysis instead gives one equal contribution to each available record.
Its country score is the sum of the selected record scores divided by that country's record
count. Equivalently, each source-family mean is weighted by its actual share of records.

For example, India's displayed selection has 27 news and 1,442 expression records. Its news
mean still receives 75 percent of the blend, showing why a record count alone cannot describe
a source's influence. The later much larger selection uses a different rule.

**Latest score = (news count × news mean + expression count × expression mean) / total count.**

# Delaunay and Gabriel: geometry within a shared category

![Emotional connections in the exhibition](assets/exhibition-network.png)

The illustrated anger network identifies the active category, geographic connections, and a
country in focus. Countries enter a category's network through the displayed emotional winner.
The edges are then constructed from geographic positions. This separates the semantic
criterion for belonging to a group from the spatial criterion for connecting its members.

## What a graph means here

A **graph** contains nodes and edges. Here the nodes stand for country locations and edges
organize proximity within a shared category. An edge is not a measured correlation between
time series, an inferred causal effect, a social tie, or evidence of psychological transmission.
Two countries can share a category without sharing a history or having a direct connection.

The current exhibition uses a spherical Delaunay construction. Its positions are chosen country
label locations, with adjustments for small places, rather than mathematically calculated
country centroids. Conceptually, its triangular mesh can be obtained from the convex hull
of the category's positions in three dimensions.
The **convex hull** is the tight outer shell around a set of points. Its triangular faces
provide neighbouring connections on the sphere. The use of a globe avoids treating longitude
as an ordinary flat coordinate with an artificial break at the map's edge.

## Gabriel as the requested comparison

A Gabriel connection uses an empty-region test. For two positions A and B, form a ball whose
diameter is the straight segment joining them. Their direct edge is retained only when no
other participating position lies inside that ball. In a flat diagram the ball appears as a
circle. The spherical version tests positions embedded in three dimensions, with chord
geometry defining the ball.

If M is the midpoint of A and B, the interior test asks whether another position C has
distance(C, M) smaller than distance(A, B)/2. A nearby point in that interior makes the
direct A-B connection unnecessary under the Gabriel rule. This is a geometric definition,
not a judgement about how similar the countries' emotions are.

[[DIAGRAM:graph]]

Delaunay generally supplies a denser proximity mesh; Gabriel supplies a more selective one.
For the current all-category view, anger has 152 nodes and 450 Delaunay edges; the compared
Gabriel construction retains 279. Hurt has 18 nodes and 48 versus 30 edges; hardship has
six nodes and 12 versus seven. These are construction counts, not relationship strengths.

The project's small-set rule matters: a three-country category uses all three pairwise edges
in either mode, without applying the Gabriel interior test. A one-country category has no
edge. Thus the mathematical Gabriel definition and the project's small-set treatment should
not be conflated. The alternative is shown here for comparison; Delaunay remains the current
exhibition network.

The empty-region definition depends on which positions participate in the comparison.
Changing the visible category membership can therefore change geometric connections even
when the country locations remain fixed. Curved drawing and animation make the connections
legible on the globe; they do not add evidence of transmission, direction, speed, or chronology.

# Interpretation, uncertainty, and questions worth keeping open

## More records and more information

**Effective sample size** means the amount of independent information available for a particular
inference. It can be smaller than the record count when articles repeat an event, publishers
share material, or authors contribute multiple posts. Exact duplicate removal helps with one
form of repetition but does not remove thematic or organizational dependence. A million
articles do not automatically supply a million independent observations of social experience.

No population confidence intervals are established by the country means in this guide.
Treating all records as independent draws from residents would underestimate uncertainty and
misidentify the target population. Statistical uncertainty around a textual mean also differs
from uncertainty about whether the metric captures the intended human construct.

## Association, causation, and the ecological leap

An **ecological inference** uses group-level observations to make a claim about individuals.
The leap is unsafe when a country's average or maximum is assigned to every resident. A
country with a strong health signal need not have uniformly high individual risk. A country
with an anger label need not contain a high proportion of angry residents.

Co-location of two layers does not identify causation. Economic conditions, health, climate,
and language may interact, but their source periods and geographic supports differ. Estimating
a relationship would first require a specified question, compatible measurements, appropriate
comparison groups, and attention to alternative explanations. The artwork invites those
questions without already answering them statistically.

## Five useful expert questions

- **What is the target of the claim?** A set of texts, a publisher system, a population,
  or a lived experience would each require a different evidential basis.
- **What changes under another aggregation?** A mean, maximum, source-balanced result,
  and record-weighted result emphasize different features of the same material.
- **Whose absence matters?** Missing countries, poorly covered languages, excluded publishers,
  and people who do not post leave different gaps.
- **How stable is a visible label?** A winning category can depend on source composition,
  permitted categories, time selection, and model calibration.
- **What would support a causal story?** Context can motivate a hypothesis, but explanatory
  attribution needs evidence beyond an evocative spatial coincidence.

An artistic framing can emphasize mutual dependence and care. A scientific framing can
emphasize measurement and uncertainty. An everyday framing can begin with movement, home,
work, or concern for another person. These are different ways to encounter the same evidence,
with room for visitors to contribute meaning without being treated as data points.
